# Netflix (NFLX): fundamentals and analysis

*Prices intraday on 2026-11-12 (about $63.87); SEC filings through 2026-09-30 (Q3 2026
shareholder letter of 20 Oct and the 10-Q of 21 Oct). Written 2026-11-12.*

## 1. Summary

**Netflix is a fast grower that is slowing down. Revenue growth fell from 17% to 12% this
year and the stock is down about 45% from its high, so it now trades at about 22x adjusted
earnings: the lowest outside the 2022 crash, against a 5-year median of about 41x. The
balance sheet is strong and free cash flow is about $11–12bn a year. View: near the buy
level. The proposed entry is 20x adjusted EPS, about $58 now and about $61 after the
Q4 report on Jan 19. A small first position at today's price is reasonable; add below $52
(17x).**

- **Cheap:** 21.9x adjusted EPS of $2.92, 16.9x 2027 consensus, PEG about 1.1. The market
  price implies about 8% a year free-cash-flow growth for ten years, against 12% revenue
  growth and 20% operating-income growth this year.
- **Strong:** passes every strict debt rule (net debt $6.9bn, about 0.4x real EBITDA),
  record buybacks ($4.8bn in Q3, about 3.4% fewer shares in a year), 31.5% operating margin
  guided for 2026.
- **Weak:** growth is slowing every quarter, advertising is behind plan ($2.7bn vs $3bn),
  viewing hours are flat, and $25bn of content commitments sit mostly off the balance sheet.
- **Uncertain:** whether 2027 brings 10–11% revenue growth with rising margins (consensus:
  EPS +24%) or a further slowdown that turns Netflix into a stalwart worth 18–20x.

operating income plus ordinary depreciation (about $0.35bn) instead.

### DCF

**The standard DCF does not work for Netflix.** It starts from operating cash flow minus
D&A, and Netflix's D&A includes $17bn of content amortization that is already paid in
operating cash flow, so the base is −$4.9bn. Deducting it twice is wrong.

The right base is free cash flow plus after-tax interest: $12.0bn (FCF $11.4bn, interest
about $0.8bn at 21% tax). At the project's CAPM WACC of 9.3%:

| Case | Fair value per share | vs price |
|---|---|---|
| 2.5% growth forever, no growth stage (the standard method's assumption) | $41.69 | −35% |
| 5% a year for 10 years, then 2.5% | $50.58 | −21% |
| 8% a year for 10 years, then 2.5% | $63.76 | 0% |
| 10% a year for 10 years, then 2.5% | $74.37 | +16% |
| Growth fading from 13% to 3% over 10 years, then 2.5% | $65.94 | +3% |
| 12% a year for 10 years, then 2.5% | $86.68 | +36% |

In reverse, **today's price implies about 8% free-cash-flow growth a year for ten years**.
The 10%-growth case is $66 at a 10% WACC and $85 at 8.5%.

## 3. Analysis

### Lynch category: a fast grower, slowing

Stage 1 of the screener puts Netflix in **fast growers** and it passes that rule set.
Adjusted EPS grew 34% in 2025 and the median of the last three years is 28%, above Lynch's
20–25% band. But revenue growth is falling every quarter (17.6% to a guided 11.5%), and
2026 adjusted EPS grows only about 14% ($2.67 to $3.04) because of Q4's job-cut charge and
the advertising shortfall. Operating income still grows 20%+ and buybacks take out about
3% of shares a year, which is how EPS can keep growing faster than revenue.

Lynch's warning for fast growers is the moment growth stops; he says to hold them while the
story is intact and sell when the expansion is clearly over. Netflix is not there: revenue
still grows double digits in every region, churn improved, and price increases were
accepted. It is moving from "fast" toward "stalwart", and the P/E has already moved with it.

The PEG ratio (Lynch's test: P/E no higher than the growth rate) is about 1.1 on 20%
growth (operating income in 2026, EPS in 2027 consensus), and 1.6 on 2026's 14%. Lynch
calls a PEG near 1 fairly priced and 0.5 a bargain, so Netflix is fair to cheap, not a
bargain.

### Why it fell

The stock is down about 45% from $115.75 (52-week high) and 52% from the June 2025 peak
of $133.91 (split-adjusted):

- revenue growth slowing quarter by quarter since Q4 2025, and 2026 revenue guidance
  trimmed to $51.1bn;
- the 2026 advertising forecast cut from about $3bn to $2.7bn (slower ad-product rollout);
- worries about flat viewing hours (a Bloomberg report in late October) and competition
  from YouTube and short video;
- the WBD bid (December 2025 to February 2026) raised fears of a large, debt-funded deal;
  it failed and Netflix was paid $2.8bn;
- Q4 guidance of $0.67 EPS against $0.73 consensus, including about 1,000 job cuts.

The results themselves are good: Q3 operating income +37%, margin 34.6%, and member churn
better than forecast.

Babak).** That is about $58.40 today, about 9% below the price, and about $60.80 after the
Q4 report if EPS meets the $0.68 consensus. 20x is a PEG of 1 on 20% growth, below the
21.2x three-year low and well above the 15x panic low of 2022.

**View.** Netflix is close to the buy level. A small first position at about $64 is
reasonable, since the price already implies only 8% cash-flow growth; the main buy is at
or below $58–61 (20x), and below about $52 (17x) if 2027 guidance disappoints. The case
to wait is that growth has slowed for four quarters running and nothing yet shows it has
stopped slowing.

### Risks

- **Slowing growth:** 2027 consensus has revenue +10.7% to $56.6bn. A guide below 10%
  would make Netflix a stalwart, and 18x would then be a fair multiple (about $55 on 2026
  EPS).
- **Engagement:** viewing hours are flat; Netflix argues price increases and churn show
  value, and wants view-hour growth back. YouTube (GOOGL) takes more TV time than Netflix
  in the US (Nielsen).
- **Advertising:** behind plan in 2026; 15 new ad-tier countries from March 2027.
- **Content commitments:** $25.2bn ($11.8bn due within a year), $18.4bn not on the balance
  sheet. They are a fixed cost if subscriber growth stalls.
- **Live sports and M&A:** NFL games, boxing and the failed WBD bid show appetite for
  costly content and deals; management still says "selective M&A".
- **Competitors** combining: Paramount Skydance now owns WBD (HBO Max), and Disney licenses
  some content to Netflix.

### What to watch

- Q4 results and the 2027 outlook around Jan 19, 2027: revenue growth (above or below
  10%), operating margin target, ad revenue, and the free
  cash flow guide.
- Buybacks: $4.8bn in Q3, $22.3bn authorization left; at today's price that is about 8% of
  the company.
- View hours and the ad-tier launches in March 2027.

## Caveats

- Netflix reports no adjusted EPS; adjusted here means GAAP minus the two one-offs above.
  GAAP EPS also includes foreign-exchange remeasurement of its euro bonds, which is small
  now (−$28m in nine months) but large in some past quarters (Q4 2022 EPS was $0.01), so
  the older yearly P/Es move with it.
- The history before 2023 uses split-adjusted SEC share counts (10-for-1, November 2025).
- Yahoo's EBITDA, EV / EBITDA and net debt / EBITDA for Netflix include content
  amortization, so the screens flatter Netflix against peers whose content costs sit
  elsewhere.
- DIS TTM runs to June 2026 (its Q4 FY26 is not yet in Yahoo's data); META's reported EPS is
  Yahoo's and may include tax items; PSKY is loss-making on GAAP after the WBD deal.
- Yahoo data is unofficial. Q3 figures come from Netflix's shareholder letter (8-K, exhibit
  99.1) and the 10-Q on SEC EDGAR.
