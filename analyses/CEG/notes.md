## Summary

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-28 -->
- **Cheap:** not yet; 18.8x adjusted earnings, below its median since 2022 and well below the 32.5x of 2025.
- **Strong:** the largest US nuclear fleet, 20-year contracts with Microsoft (Crane restart) and Meta, and adjusted EPS of about $12.65 over 12 months (up from $5.34 in 2023), at the top of 2026 guidance ($12.50-12.80).
- **Weak:** total debt rose from $9.0bn to $23.4bn and cash fell from $3.6bn to $0.6bn with Calpine; free cash flow is only about $1.3bn against $4.4bn of capex.
- **Uncertain:** how much of the record Q3 ($5.06 a share) comes from power and capacity prices that can fall again, and from PJM market rules and politics.

## Business

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-28 -->
Constellation, spun off from Exelon in February 2022, owns the largest fleet of US nuclear plants and, since buying Calpine in early 2026, a large fleet of gas plants; it also sells power and gas to businesses and homes. Revenue over 12 months is $34.3bn and EBITDA $9.8bn. Q3 2026 adjusted operating EPS was $5.06 (GAAP $5.19), against $3.04 a year earlier and about $3.90 expected, from Calpine, higher PJM capacity prices and strong trading; it bought back about $3bn of stock in 2026, $0.8bn in Q3 ([Constellation](https://www.constellationenergy.com/news/2026/11/constellation-reports-third-quarter-2026-results.html)). Yahoo's quarterly EPS is the company's adjusted figure, so no correction is needed. Q3 is the strongest quarter (summer demand), so the 12-month EPS is the right base, not Q3 times four. The P/E history starts in 2022, so the "10-year" median covers four years.

Two reading notes on the numbers. Operating cash flow was negative in 2021-2024 because cash collected on sold customer receivables is reported under investing, not operations; free cash flow for those years understates the business. For the same reason the DCF fair value ($47) is not meaningful.

## Risks

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-28 -->
- **Power prices:** earnings above the contracted base move with wholesale power, gas and PJM capacity prices; the Calpine gas fleet makes them more cyclical.
- **Debt:** net debt about $22.8bn, about 2.3x EBITDA, with interest costs already weighing on Q3.
- **Politics and market rules:** PJM capacity price caps, state pressure on power bills and rules on plants serving data centres directly.
- **Data-centre demand:** the 2024-25 rerating rested on AI power demand; a slowdown in data-centre building would hit the multiple first.
- **Nuclear operations:** an outage, or delays to the Crane restart (targeted for the second half of 2027) and the uprates.
