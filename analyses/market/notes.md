## Verdict

**October ended with a sharp tech sell-off: the S&P 500 fell 1.8% and the Nasdaq about 3% on Oct 30, their worst day since June, on doubts that heavy AI spending will pay off. The index is only about 3% below its high, so this is a pullback, not a panic; the bargains are still in the sold-off software and data names.**

## Summary

- **Indices:** the S&P 500 is about 3% below its high and up about 10.5% this year; the Nasdaq is about 8% below its high after its worst month since March 2025.
- **Pressure:** oil is near $103 and the US 10-year yield near 4.9%, both up sharply this year.
- **Fear:** the VIX jumped about 13% to about 19.4, still a normal level.
- **Opportunity:** software and data names sold off on AI fears still have growing earnings and are near decade-low valuations.

## What is driving the market

On Oct 30 the S&P 500 fell about 1.8% to about 7,580 and the Nasdaq about 2.9%, the worst day for both since June; the Dow lost about 1.1%. Intel fell about 29%, its worst day since 1974, on weak fourth-quarter guidance, after Alphabet's results (Oct 28) and other tech reports raised doubts about the return on AI spending. Earlier in the week, on Oct 28, the S&P 500 was about 7,747, under 1% from its high. On Oct 27 the S&P 500 rose about 0.7% to about 7,730 and the Nasdaq about 1.3%, led by chips and Big Tech ahead of their earnings and this week's Fed meeting. Oil topped $100 a barrel in September on Middle East supply worries, and the 10-year yield broke 5% for the first time since 2023 on inflation fears and the chance of Fed rate rises. The index has held up because the largest companies keep growing earnings, but breadth is middling: a little under 60% of S&P 500 stocks are above their 200-day average.

## Stocks to look at

173 of the 497 S&P 500 companies pass Stage 1 (good financial statements for their Lynch category), and 27 of those are cheap or close to cheap: the top 3 in each of 13 sectors.

- **Cheapest against their own history:** Adobe, Zoetis, Allstate, Intuit and First Solar sit about 40-55% below their cheap price. Adobe and Intuit are the same AI-fear names the October 25 screen found, now confirmed by the statement checks.
- **Check before trusting:** Allstate (5.4x) and Comcast (6.2x) look cheap because recent earnings are unusually high (a light catastrophe year; one-off gains), and Valero's refining margins are at a peak with oil near $100. Cyclicals look cheapest exactly when earnings peak.
- **On the watch list too:** Booking is in the list (fast grower, about 15% below its cheap price on adjusted EPS). Alphabet and Uber, new on the watch list, are not.

Each name still needs step 1 (absolute values with history) before any decision.
