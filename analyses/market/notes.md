## Verdict

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
**An AI-led sell-off, not a crash. The S&P 500 has fallen five sessions running to about 7,324 (Nov 18 close), 6% below its high; the Nasdaq is 11% below its high. Investors are selling the companies that build and buy AI computing (chips, data centres, cloud) while the rest of the market holds up much better. For a long-term buyer this is when good companies hit their entry prices: Booking has, and Netflix is 3% away.**

## Summary

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
- **Indices:** the S&P 500 is 6% below its high, still up about 7% this year; the Nasdaq is 11% below its high and down about 8% in a month.
- **Cause:** worries about the return on AI spending, a new US rule on AI-chip exports (Nov 16) and turmoil at Nvidia after its CEO resigned.
- **Fear:** the VIX is near 22, raised but well short of panic (above 30).
- **Opportunity:** software and data names sold off on AI fears (Adobe, Gartner, Intuit) still grow earnings and are 45-60% below their cheap price.

## What happened

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
- **Nov 16:** the S&P 500 fell 1.9% to 7,417 and the chip index 5.3%, the worst day since Nov 5 ([AP via WTOP](https://wtop.com/national/2026/11/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-monday-11-16-2026)).
- **Nov 17:** the S&P 500 fell another 1% to 7,341 and the Dow 1.6%, with Nvidia and other AI stocks leading the losses.
- **Nov 18:** a split day: the S&P 500 slipped 0.2% to 7,324, its fifth loss in a row and the longest losing streak since June, while the Dow lost 1.1% and the Nasdaq rose 0.4% ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/11/18/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq/f0208c14-e395-11f1-a99e-b3e3805c6b8b_story.html)). Nvidia beat its forecasts but closed 2.9% lower, while AMD (+5.3%) and Broadcom (+3.7%) rose ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/11/18/stock-market-today-nov-18-nvidia-slips-on-strong-results-as-succession-and-china-business-risks-weigh/)).
- **Rates and commodities:** the US 10-year yield is about 5.1%; oil fell about 20% in a month to about $83; gold and bitcoin are down too, so investors are not fleeing into safe havens, just out of crowded tech.

## Why it happened

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
Three things came together:

1. **Doubts about AI spending.** The largest tech companies spend hundreds of billions of dollars a year on AI data centres. After Alphabet's and Meta's results in late October, investors began asking when that spending pays back. The US midterm elections (Nov 3) are over, but their result still matters: Democrats won control of Congress, and investors reassessed the data-centre build-out in the days after ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/ai-data-center-midterms-selloff.html)). That set off the Nov 5 rout, the worst day since April 2025.
2. **A new chip-export rule.** On Nov 16 the Commerce Department required licences for advanced AI chips sent to data centres that serve companies in embargoed countries such as China ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/16/business/stocks-nvidia-ai-anthropic)). That cuts into Nvidia's and other chipmakers' sales.
3. **Nvidia's leadership.** Jensen Huang resigned as CEO the week before; Colette Kress is interim CEO, and both face congressional interviews. Strong results (revenue $115.8bn, +103%, and a $130bn Q4 guide) were not enough to lift the stock ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)).

Meanwhile Anthropic's IPO, priced at $83 on Nov 18 and the largest ever at $81bn raised, shows money is still willing to pay for AI, at a price ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-ipo-ai-offering-price.html)).

## What it means

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
In plain terms: the market is not falling because companies earn less. It is falling because investors are paying less for each dollar of future AI profit. The damage is concentrated: the equal-weighted S&P 500 fell about half as much as the index on Nov 16, so the typical company is doing much better than the giants.

For a Lynch-style investor that matters in two ways:

- **Good businesses get cheaper with the crowd.** Booking and Netflix have little to do with AI chips, but they fell with the market and are now at or near their entry prices. That is the opportunity in a sell-off like this.
- **Don't confuse cheap with safe.** Nvidia (18x) and the chip names now screen as cheap, but their earnings are cyclical and depend on spending that is being questioned. Cyclicals look cheapest when earnings peak.

With the VIX near 22 and 57% of S&P 500 stocks still above their 200-day average, this is a correction in a crowded corner of the market, not a broad panic. It could go further if AI spending is actually cut, which would show up first in the big tech companies' 2027 capex plans (late January).
