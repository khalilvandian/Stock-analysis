## Why it happened

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-20 -->
The big new reason is **interest rates**. The minutes of the Fed's October meeting, published on Nov 18, showed almost all officials expect further rate hikes; two of them had already voted for a hike in October ([CNBC](https://cnbc.com/2026/11/18/fed-minutes-october-2026.html)). Governor Waller said a half-point hike may be needed at the Dec 8-9 meeting, though New York Fed President Williams said the situation doesn't call for dramatic action ([Axios](https://axios.com/2026/11/19/interest-rates-ai-fed-october)). Markets now expect at least a quarter-point hike in December.

Higher rates hit share prices in two ways: borrowing costs more for companies, and future profits are worth less today, which hurts growth stocks most. That is why the sell-off, which began with AI and chip stocks (doubts about the return on AI spending, a new chip-export rule on Nov 16 and Nvidia's leadership turmoil), spread on Thursday to the whole market. Anthropic's strong debut was not enough to change the mood.

## What it means

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-20 -->
In plain terms: until this week investors worried about how much to pay for AI profits. Now they also worry that money will get more expensive for everyone. That is a broader reason to sell, and it explains why stocks with nothing to do with AI (Booking, Uber, Netflix) fell as much as the tech giants on Thursday.

For a Lynch-style investor:

- **The businesses haven't changed; the prices have.** None of the watch list's companies reported bad news this week. Their earnings estimates are the same; only the multiple the market pays has fallen.
- **Prefer strong balance sheets while rates rise.** Booking (more cash than debt) and Netflix (modest debt) are better placed than companies borrowing heavily, such as Boston Scientific with its Penumbra deal.
- **Buy in steps.** After six down days, the market can fall further before the Fed meets on Dec 8-9. Spreading purchases over several weeks avoids having to guess the bottom.
