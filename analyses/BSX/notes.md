## Verdict

**Q3 (Oct 28) missed on earnings and the year's outlook was cut a fourth time: sales grew only 1.5% and are guided to fall in Q4. The stock is cheap (about 13x) and now about 2% above the $41.5 entry, but growth has stalled rather than slowed. View: a small first position only at or below $41.5; wait for the 2027 outlook (late January) before adding.**

## Summary

- **Cheap:** the lowest valuation in a decade, and cheaper than most medical-device peers.
- **Strong:** the best profitability and cash generation in its peer group.
- **Weak:** little cash against its debt, with more debt coming from the Penumbra deal.
- **Uncertain:** growth has fallen from about 16% to about 4% for 2026, with Q4 sales guided down 1–3%; how much is the cyberattack and how much is lasting share loss is not yet clear.

## Business

Boston Scientific makes devices for heart, vascular, digestive and urology procedures. The growth engines of the last few years were electrophysiology (pulsed field ablation, Farapulse) and the Watchman left-atrial-appendage closure device. Organic growth was 15.8% in 2025; the 2026 guide is now about 3.75% after four cuts (Q3 2026: +1.5%). It generates about $3.6bn of free cash flow a year (SEC capex).

## Risks

- **Share loss in US electrophysiology:** competitors' pulsed field ablation systems are taking share.
- **Watchman:** stand-alone Watchman procedures are falling.
- **Debt:** the pending $14.5bn Penumbra deal adds about $11bn of debt, taking net debt to EBITDA near 3.8x; it also needs an FTC remedy.
- **Cyberattack:** the Aug 25 attack disrupted manufacturing and shipping; likely a one-off but it clouds Q3.
