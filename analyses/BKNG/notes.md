## Verdict

**Booking is at its entry price. At $154.9 (Nov 18 close) it trades at 15.1x adjusted earnings, against a 10-year norm of about 21x and the $154 entry (15x). ## Today

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
The stock closed at $154.9 on Nov 18, down about 3% since Nov 13 and about 10% below the $171 it reached the day after its Q3 report (Nov 4). There was no company news behind the fall. Two things pulled it down:

- **The market:** the S&P 500 fell about 3% in five sessions, led by AI and chip stocks, and investors sold most growth stocks with them.
- **The Q4 guide is sinking in:** analysts at Jefferies, RBC and Wedbush cut their targets after Q3 (all still $180-210), because the company guided Q4 room nights to only +1-3%.

**What it means for you:** at 15x, the price assumes growth slows for good. If room nights recover to mid-single digits in 2027, the stock is cheap here; if AI booking agents start taking customers, 15x is fair, not cheap. The entry rule says buy, and nothing in this week's news changes the case. The insider sale (50,000 shares) is small next to Booking's roughly $4bn of buybacks a quarter.

## Summary

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
- **Cheap:** 15.1x adjusted earnings, at the 15x entry and well below its 10-year norm of about 21x.
- **Strong:** passes every strict debt rule and generates about $9.5bn of free cash flow a year.
- **Weak:** negative shareholders' equity because of buybacks.
- **Uncertain:** Q4 room nights are guided to just +1-3%, and AI agents and regulators (a $900m FTC charge in Q3) are open risks.

## Business

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
Booking runs Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK and OpenTable. Most of its profit comes from hotel and alternative-accommodation commissions, mainly in Europe. Results are still growing: Q3 2026 adjusted EPS of $4.66 beat the $4.50 consensus and grew 17%. It returns about 10% of its market value to shareholders each year through buybacks and dividends. The stock split 25-for-1 in April 2026.

## Risks

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-19 -->
- **AI agents:** assistants that book travel directly (Meta's Muse agent, Sep 23) could cut Booking out of the customer relationship.
- **Slowing volume:** room-night growth fell from 8–9% in 2025 to 5% in Q2 and a guided 3–5% in Q3.
- **Regulators:** an EU Digital Services Act probe (Oct 21), reported FTC action over affiliate hotel ads (Oct 7), and the EU court upholding the ETraveli block (Sep 9).
