## Today

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-20 -->
Booking fell about 4.9% on Nov 19 to $147.3, more than the S&P 500's 2% fall, and is now 28% below its August high.

- **Why:** there was no company news. The whole market fell after the Fed's minutes showed almost all officials expect more rate hikes ([CNBC](https://cnbc.com/2026/11/18/fed-minutes-october-2026.html)). Travel stocks fell further on worries about consumer travel spending and US flight cancellations; Expedia is down 24% from August too ([TradersUnion](https://tradersunion.com/news/stocks/show/4327942-booking-holdings-slides-3-5percent-to/)).
- **The known weak spot:** management guided Q4 room nights to only +1-3%.

**What it means for you:** this is the situation the entry price was set for. You are paying 14.3x for a company that grew adjusted EPS 17% last quarter and buys back about 10% of its shares' value each year. Higher interest rates hurt the price of growth stocks, but not Booking's cash flow, since it holds more cash than debt. The risk is that travel demand really is slowing, which Q4 will show. Buying in two or three steps spreads that risk.

## Summary

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-20 -->
- **Cheap:** 14.3x adjusted earnings, 5% below the 15x entry and well below its 10-year norm of about 21x.
- **Strong:** passes every strict debt rule and generates about $9.5bn of free cash flow a year.
- **Weak:** negative shareholders' equity because of buybacks.
- **Uncertain:** Q4 room nights are guided to just +1-3%, and AI agents and regulators (a $900m FTC charge in Q3) are open risks.

## Business

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-20 -->
Booking runs Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK and OpenTable. Most of its profit comes from hotel and alternative-accommodation commissions, mainly in Europe. Results are still growing: Q3 2026 adjusted EPS of $4.66 beat the $4.50 consensus and grew 17%. It returns about 10% of its market value to shareholders each year through buybacks and dividends. The stock split 25-for-1 in April 2026.

## Risks

<!-- reviewed 2026-11-20 -->
- **AI agents:** assistants that book travel directly (Meta's Muse agent, Sep 23) could cut Booking out of the customer relationship.
- **Slowing volume:** room-night growth fell from 8–9% in 2025 to 5% in Q2 and a guided 3–5% in Q3.
- **Regulators:** an EU Digital Services Act probe (Oct 21), reported FTC action over affiliate hotel ads (Oct 7), and the EU court upholding the ETraveli block (Sep 9).
